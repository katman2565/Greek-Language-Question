# Comparing the Histories of the Greek Language Question: Towards a Computer-Assisted Analysis
A computational methodology to analyze three key histories of the Greek Language Question. We examine origins and periodizations, the separation of historical narration from quotation, the evaluative framing of mentioned scholars, recurrent themes and emotions, semantic search and corpus-specific word embeddings. The analysis traces the historians’ citation practices, showing how earlier ideas are enregistered in their narratives, creating an ideological space for what came to be known as The Greek Language Question. A clear shift emerges: Sathas surveys diverse views on what appear to be distinct “language questions”; he also presupposes a broad diaglossic continuum of at least five varieties (pentaglossia). By contrast, Megas and Kordatos, despite their differences, both assume a rigid diglossic split between “high” and “low” varieties; and they also think that the Greek Language Question is all about this split.
